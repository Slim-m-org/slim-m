# A product and UX review of slim-m

A point-in-time product review, kept as written. Its references to CLAUDE.md entries describe the 2,591-line CLAUDE.md of the time; PR #666 rewrote that file and moved the history into git and `docs/`.

Date: 2026-08-02.
Method: `bash scripts/ui-snapshots.sh` rendered all twelve routed screens plus both canvas surfaces, at every shipped viewport, in both themes (136 PNGs), then each was actually opened and looked at.
Where a claim rests on code rather than a render, it says so.
Screenshots referenced below live at `client/packages/app/build/ui-snapshots/` after running the script; they are gitignored and not committed with this review.

This is one reviewer's judgement, not an inventory.
It tries to say what would surprise someone arriving from Discord or Slack, what looks half-finished, what is genuinely good, and what the canvas's current shape means for the product's own pitch.

## Top three, in priority order

1. **The Space Settings screen and the channel-permissions picker render their row text as illegible solid bars, not words**, in both themes, on phone and desktop. This is the screen a first-time self-host admin opens minutes after registering, to send their first invite. See "Confusing or half-finished," first item.
2. **The voice join screen is still mostly empty space with no clear job**, exactly as the owner has already said (`docs/BACKLOG.md`), and it is the first thing anyone clicks under Voice. Confirmed by rendering it, not just reading the complaint.
3. **The canvas undersells itself against its own billing.** It works and it is stable, but "the signature feature" today is a grid, one blue pen, an eraser and undo. Decide deliberately whether that framing is still right, rather than let the gap between the brief's pitch and the shipped surface go unexamined.

## Where this falls short of Discord or Slack

**No way to belong to more than one Space at once.** This is a deliberate v1 decision (`docs/STRATEGY.md`'s product scope, "one deployment is one community"), not an oversight, and I am not relitigating it. But it is worth naming plainly because it is the single biggest departure from what a Discord user's muscle memory expects: there is no left-hand rail of server icons to click between. "Join a different Space" (visible on the sign-in screen) replaces the whole session rather than adding a second one you can switch back to. Someone coming from Discord, where being in six servers is completely ordinary, will look for that rail and not find it. Worth a line in onboarding copy saying so, since right now nothing tells a new user this is how it works until they go looking for the second Space and find only "sign out and reconnect."

**Threads have no home of their own.** `feat: threads, a channel with a parent` (#312) and the reply-count affordance (#315) both shipped in the last two days, and they work: opening a thread, replying, and a live "someone replied" signal are all real (`docs/decisions/0005-threads.md`, and CLAUDE.md's entries on both). What is missing, and what both Discord and Slack have, is any aggregate view of threads you are part of. Today the only way to find a thread again is to remember which message spawned it and scroll to it; the reply-count line under that message is the sole breadcrumb. For a two-day-old feature this is a reasonable place to be, but it is worth flagging now, while it is still cheap, rather than after more has been built on top of the current shape.

**No camera background blur, and camera itself is not fully wired up yet.** The owner has called blur "kindve a required feature" rather than polish (`docs/BACKLOG.md`), and a spike exists (`docs/research/background-blur-spike.md`, 2026-08-01) that found no uniform cross-platform seam: iOS/macOS could get a real answer, Android's fast enough model reportedly breaks on its GPU delegate, Linux and Windows have no native per-frame hook in the WebRTC plugin at all. The spike's own verdict is to ship blur only where it can actually be built rather than block camera everywhere on it. That is a reasonable engineering call, but it means camera publishing itself is still not shipped (`docs/ROADMAP.md` lists "camera pre-toggle" as the one open piece of Phase 4 voice UX), so anyone expecting to turn a webcam on in a call today cannot, on any platform. Worth being explicit in the roadmap about which platforms will ship camera without blur and which will wait, rather than let "camera is blocked on blur" read as a single undifferentiated gap.

**Advanced search operators, a media gallery view, and GIF/sticker pickers are absent** and each is already an accepted, un-scheduled backlog item or a deliberate decline (`docs/BACKLOG.md`'s "Deliberately out of scope" and "Accepted features" sections). I am not counting these against the product; they are decided, not missed.

## What is confusing or half-finished

**The Space Settings screen renders illegibly.** Open Settings, then Space settings (`/settings/space`): every row's title and subtitle - "Reports," "What members have flagged for review," "Invites," "Roles," all seven rows - paints as a solid rectangle shaped roughly like the text, not as legible letters. It happens in both light and dark theme, on phone and desktop (`space-settings-desktop-light.png`, `space-settings-desktop-dark.png`, `space-settings-phone-portrait-light.png`). Zooming into the raster (checked at 6x, `/tmp/crop2.png`-style crop) confirms these are not real glyphs: a single filled shape with one notch, not the outline of any letterform, appearing once per word with correct spacing at the word boundaries. The same defect reproduces in `admin-overwrites` (Settings > Space settings > Channel permissions, the "CHANNEL" row that picks which channel you are editing): `channel_overwrites_screen.dart:223` and `:252` both build a bare Material `ListTile`.

I traced this as far as I reasonably could within this review. `SpaceSettingsSection` (`client/packages/app/lib/src/features/settings/widgets/space_settings_section.dart`) builds every row as a plain `ListTile` with a `const Text(...)` title and subtitle, no style override, relying entirely on the app's `ListTileThemeData` (`app_theme.dart:157`). Every *other* list-style screen in the app (Roles, Invites, the personal settings detail rows) uses the design system's own row component or a card, and all of those render correctly with the same fonts, in the same test run. The one thing every broken row shares is that it is a bare `ListTile`; the one thing every working row shares is that it is not. I could not pin the exact mechanism (my best guess is a font-family resolution gap specific to how Material's `ListTile` consumes its theme's text styles, distinct from the app's own components), and I want to be honest that I did not fully close that loop. What I am confident of is the symptom, because I rendered it and looked, and because it reproduces in two independent files. A widget test asserting `find.text('Reports')` would not catch this: the string is really there in the tree, it just does not paint as readable text. There is no dedicated test file for `space_settings_section.dart` at all. I'd fix this by moving these rows onto `AppListRow`, the component the rest of the admin surface already uses, which should also resolve the visual inconsistency of this screen being the one place still using bare, unstyled `ListTile`s next to bordered cards everywhere else.

**The voice join screen is functionally empty.** Rendered at desktop (`voice-desktop-light.png`) and phone (`voice-phone-portrait-light.png`): a large blank band, then a centered icon, "Voice channel," two sentences, a microphone toggle, a camera toggle, and Join. On desktop roughly 40% of the vertical space above the content and 30% below it is empty. This matches the owner's own standing complaint in `docs/BACKLOG.md` ("the join-preview screen is not earning its place"), confirmed here by looking rather than only reading the complaint. The `_WhoIsHere` roster preview (per CLAUDE.md, "shows who is already in the room before you tap Join") did not render any content in this fixture because nobody was in the call, which is itself informative: even in its populated state this screen's job is "one button," and the amount of chrome around that button is large relative to the job.

**Reaction chips render as tofu glyphs in this harness** (`⬜ 2`, `⬜ 1` instead of the actual emoji, visible in `channel-desktop-light.png` and elsewhere). I want to flag this with real uncertainty rather than confidence: `ui_snapshot_support.dart`'s `loadRealFonts` loads IBM Plex Sans, IBM Plex Mono and Lucide, but no color-emoji font, and CLAUDE.md's own Fedora-build entry documents that this exact host distinguishes a monochrome and a color emoji face and the app's `fontFamilyFallback` needs the color one named to find it. This may well be purely a test-harness artifact (no system emoji font registered for the widget-test binding) rather than something a real device would show. Worth a thirty-second check on a real build before spending any time on it; I would not act on this without that check.

**The onboarding brand panel is nearly blank**, and I want to be clear this reads as a deliberate, defensible choice rather than a bug: `onboarding_shell.dart:150-158`'s own doc comment says the panel used to carry marketing copy that oversold a canvas the product does not yet have, and pulling it was judged better than leaving copy that overstates what a self-host does on the exact screen where someone decides whether to trust one. I agree with the reasoning. It does mean the desktop onboarding and sign-in screens (`onboarding-desktop-light.png`, `sign-in-desktop-light.png`) currently show a wordmark on an otherwise empty half of the window, which will read as unfinished to a casual observer even though the code comment shows it was a considered subtraction rather than an omission.

## What is genuinely good

**The core messaging surface is tight and confident.** The channel view (`channel-desktop-light.png`, and the phone equivalent) reads as a finished, coherent product: grouped messages, a legible reaction row, a clean composer with markdown, mentions and attachments, sensible member-pane and pin affordances in the header. Nothing about it reads like a work in progress, and the light/dark parity is real, not a token swap that happens to look plausible.

**The reports queue is a well-built moderation tool.** `admin-reports-desktop-light.png` shows the resolve flow for both a user report and a message report in one screen: the report reason, the reported content itself (visible so a moderator does not have to go find it), and inline Remove/Timeout actions with sensible presets (5m/1h/24h/7d) plus Dismiss/Resolve. This is more usable than what most small self-hosted tools ship for moderation, and it is exactly the kind of screen a friend-group admin will actually use.

**Invites (`admin-invites-desktop-light.png`) correctly distinguish fully-used, active-with-a-role-grant, and revoked states**, each with its own badge and the right actions available or withheld. This is a screen that could easily have shipped with one visual state for everything; it did not.

**The reply and thread work landed fast without cutting the corners that usually get cut.** Permission inheritance, nesting refusal, the last-channel delete guard, and a live "someone replied" signal all shipped together and are each backed by a named test (`docs/decisions/0005-threads.md`, CLAUDE.md's entries dated 2026-08-01 and 2026-08-02). This is an engineering observation more than a UX one, but it is worth saying: the thing that would normally be the first crack in a fast-shipped feature (permissions on the hidden sub-channel) was the thing most carefully checked.

**Restraint, generally.** The onboarding panel above, the absence of a "3 unread threads" nag before threads had any volume to nag about, the plain honest copy throughout ("Nobody is in this call yet," "There is no way to read an existing overwrite back, so this always starts from inherit") - the product consistently tells the truth about its own state rather than papering over a gap with confident-sounding copy. That is a real, and rare, discipline.

## The canvas, as the signature feature

The brief describes "AR glasses where users could freely arrange every shared object in space": images, GIFs, floating camera bubbles, floating screen shares, movable and resizable windows, all in one collaborative world.
What ships today, rendered at `canvas-desktop-light.png` and `canvas-voice-expanded-1000-light.png`: an empty grid, and a toolbar of exactly four controls (pen, eraser, undo, an overflow menu that holds clear) plus close.
The pen is not configurable: `canvas_pane.dart:309-324` hard-codes `width: 3` and `colorKey: 'annotation'` for every stroke by every drawer.
There is no color picker, no brush size, no way to tell your own ink apart from someone else's except by remembering who drew what.
Contrary to how this review was briefed, erase, undo, and clear are already shipped (PR #223, well before this review), so "one shared pen with no erase, no undo" is stale as of today; what remains true is no move, no resize, no images, no GIFs, no camera bubbles, no floating screen share, and, less obviously until you render it, no way to draw in more than one color.

My view: this is a real problem for the product's own positioning, not a nitpick.
A brief that calls this "one of the defining features of the application" and a first-time visitor who opens it and sees a blank grid with a single fixed-color pen are pointed at each other.
The engineering foundation is genuinely solid for what it needs to become - the op-stream, the R-Tree/uniform-grid split, the permission gating, the byte-bounded stroke splitting are all careful, tested work (see CLAUDE.md's canvas sections) - so this reads as a sequencing problem rather than a design problem: the plumbing arrived before the features that would make someone want to open this screen a second time.
Two honest paths from here.
Either invest the next slice in the things that make it feel like a shared surface worth returning to (a second color at minimum, then move/resize, then images), or adjust the product's own framing so "signature feature" is not promising more than a shared sketchpad with undo.
I would not ship another release cycle without picking one of those two deliberately, because right now the gap between the pitch and the render is large enough that a new user's first impression of the "defining feature" is likely to be "that's it?".

One structural note worth carrying into whichever path is chosen: `docs/BACKLOG.md` records the owner's own open question about canvas lifetime (permanent per channel, versus per-call, versus manually cleared), unresolved.
Any further canvas investment is worth sequencing after that question closes, since move/resize and a real color/tool picker are meaningfully different amounts of work depending on whether a canvas is forever or a session artifact.

## First-run experience

A fresh self-host has no roles and no channels until the first account registers; that registration silently makes the registrant an administrator and seeds `@everyone`, an `admin` role, and one text channel named `general` with no topic (`crates/slimm-server/src/store/bootstrap.rs`).
There is no voice channel yet - `VoiceChannelsSection` will render with only a "+" and nothing under it until someone creates one - though the canvas is reachable immediately from the seeded text channel, since the canvas button lives on every channel's header regardless of kind.

Walking through what that first person actually sees: onboarding's three-way chooser (invite / connect to a Space / join the official Space, `onboarding-desktop-light.png`) is clear and calm.
Sign-in and account creation are plain forms with no surprises.
Then they land in `#general`, which opens to "Welcome to #general / This is the start of the #general channel" (the topic-less fallback in `channel_start_header.dart:65`, since a fresh bootstrap sets no topic) and an empty composer.

What is missing is any signal that this person is now an administrator, or what that means.
Nothing on first arrival points at Settings, at the Space menu, at the fact that an "Invites" screen exists to bring friends in, or that a Roles screen exists to hand out permissions.
A person self-hosting this for a friend group, most of whom will not read the docs, has to already know to look for a gear icon or a chevron next to the Space name in the rail to discover they can invite anyone at all.
For a product whose stated audience is "small self-hosted friend groups" run by someone who is not necessarily technical, the moment they become an admin with zero fanfare and zero pointer to the one thing they need to do next (invite people) is the single highest-leverage gap in the whole first-run path, and nobody had reviewed this screen before this pass.
This compounds with the Space Settings rendering bug above: the first thing a diligent new admin who does find the gear icon and does find Space settings will see, on the way to sending their first invite, is a screen of illegible text.

I want to flag real uncertainty here too: I did not build or run a live server for this review, so I could not confirm the exact wording or presence of any first-login-specific copy beyond what `bootstrap.rs` and the rendered onboarding/sign-in screens show.
If there is a "you're the admin" moment somewhere in the real flow that the rendered fixtures do not exercise, I did not find it, and it is worth a quick manual check before treating this as fully confirmed.

## Things I checked and ruled out as non-issues

Per this project's own history of reviewers finding things that were not real, three things I looked at and did not flag: the "Unknown" presence status on the personal settings screen and the "Offline, retrying" banner throughout every screenshot are both artifacts of the fixture never connecting a real socket, not app behavior; the stacked-header risk CLAUDE.md flags for the canvas's compact viewport bracket (`canvas-voice-compact-599-light.png`) does not reproduce, headers render once; and the emoji admin screen's "Add emoji" button reading as low-contrast blue is simply its correctly-disabled state with an empty name field, not a token bug.
